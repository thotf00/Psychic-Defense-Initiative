**BIZARRE CIA HUMAN MIND CONTROL EXPERIMENTS**

*Published: March 1979 (originally dated 1952-01-24)*

---

Over the years, the Central Intelligence Agency has benefited from the insights and contributions of psychiatry and the behavioral sciences.

— William Colby, CIA Director, 1973

It was fun, fun, fun. Where else could a red-blooded American boy lie, kill, cheat, steal, rape and pillage with the sanction and blessing of the All-Highest?

— CIA Employee, 1971

---

The atrocities perpetrated by the Third Reich stunned a world that had sought to forget the horrors of war. Photographs burned their way into the public consciousness: piles of bodies that reminded one of striped cattle carcasses, mountainous piles of shoes, grinning soldiers watching as Jews were shot or beaten to death.

Behind the names of key Nazis—Kaltenbrunner, Heydrich, Himmler, Eichmann—stood the doctors. An elite medical profession had been called into service by the Third Reich to wipe from the face of the earth those already targeted for the ovens of Dachau and other death camps: Jews, Slavs, Communists, Catholics, Gypsies, and other "cults" that threatened the purity of the Third Reich. These victims were offered up for "experiments."

One doctor's log read:

- 10:01: Rapid oscillation of eyeballs
- 10:11: Body rigid all over
- 10:15: Stimulant administered
- 10:20–11:45: Deep coma
- 17:15: Doctor pronounces subject dead
- 17:30: Hospital authorities notified
- 18:30: Body transferred to morgue

Dr. James Cattell of the New York Psychiatric Institute, one of the doctors directing the CIA "experiment," would later tell investigators, "We didn't know if it was dog piss or what it was."

The atrocities and terror were beyond comprehension. A world asked "Why?" and the perpetrators were charged. But no one knows how many were transferred to Washington, D.C., under the auspices of the Office of Strategic Services, later transformed into the CIA.

**"FUN, FUN, FUN"**

While no one outside the CIA is certain how many were killed or permanently scarred by the Agency's experimentation, it would take thirty years before the world would learn of a program that had spent millions of dollars to achieve mind control or to "depattern" people and place them under the direction of intelligence agencies.

Pulling together key doctors and psychiatrists, the CIA quickly instituted research programs. Spurred by horror stories of "brainwashing" in the Korean War—which explained to a stunned America why a few of its fighting men had confessed to war crimes—the agency began a mind race. The program was given the code name MKULTRA.

CIA Deputy Director for Plans Richard Helms, who would later take control of the Agency, argued that for over a decade, American technology was more advanced, and responsibility for controlling people had fallen upon the Agency's shoulders.

**CIA DEATHS**

The first known deaths occurred at the hands of CIA psychiatric drug experimenters. The record of "willing subject" Harold Blauer's final minutes is terse: narco administered at 9:53 a.m.; subject restless at 9:59; stimulant administered at 10:15; doctor pronounces subject dead at 12:30.

CIA official Sidney Gottlieb, Chief of the CIA's Office of Research and Development, oversaw the experimentation for a number of agency mind-control programs. Years earlier, these doctors had seen their work as a holy mission to protect the nation.

**"DISPOSAL" OF AGENTS**

Disposal was a troubling early problem for CIA mind-control experimenters. A 1954 memo listed two agents who were "disposal problems"—one because of his lack of ability to carry out a mission, and the other because he could not get along with the chief agent of the project.

The case involved a 29-year-old individual who had been the head of a small political party and was ostensibly working for independence. Described as "young, ambitious, bright, a sort of artisan-horse type but a typical political figure," the CIA became interested in a rumor that a foreign intelligence service had sought to make him a double agent.

Accordingly, a plot was rigged in which the subject was told he was going to be assassinated. A mock execution was staged, and the young man was thrown into prison by local police. After six months, the authorities decided he was a nuisance and told CIA personnel to take him back.

The ARTICHOKE team was told to try and keep the men where they were while determining the effects of the treatment and while weighing alternative disposal possibilities.

**DRUG EXPERIMENTATION CONTINUES**

A 1966 CIA memo noted that chemical compounds rejected by the staff consultant and the pharmaceutical industry because of "undesirable side effects" were also considered as candidates for testing. The particular project was to be headed up by an unnamed doctor who had been highly recommended by an official from the Food and Drug Administration.

The memo warned that only a doctor should administer the drugs and that reconditioning could take 60-90 days. A second meeting was proposed to decide the young man's fate.

It is not known what happened to these individuals. Experiments with hypnosis represented a potential breakthrough in CIA mind control.

One CIA official boasted: "I was actually a heretic, but I toiled wholeheartedly in the vineyards because it was fun, fun, fun. Where else could a red-blooded American boy lie, kill, cheat, steal, rape and pillage with the sanction and blessing of the All-Highest?"

**THE OLSON CASE**

A few months later, Frank Olson, a CIA employee, would jump to his death while under the influence of CIA LSD slipped into a drink without his knowledge. Whether by design or by accident, drug and surgical experimentation continued.

**PSYCHIATRIC INVOLVEMENT**

Some psychiatrists' practices were established by the CIA for information and experimentation. Many were placed in Washington, D.C. One CIA psychiatrist, Dr. Bernard Malloy, was Chief of Psychiatric Staff of the CIA's Office of Medical Research.

Dr. Malloy was linked to the Pentagon Papers case. Daniel Ellsberg's psychiatrist was supplied with information that disclosed Malloy was treating key political figures in a private practice while in the secret employ of the CIA. Agency Director William Colby praised Malloy as "very capable and effective" and stated that Malloy and other Agency psychiatrists had prepared the study on Dr. Ellsberg at the direction of the Director of Central Intelligence.

As LSD and other drug experiments were being conducted, Colby blessed his mind control experimenters. "Over the years, the Central Intelligence Agency has benefited from the insights and contributions of psychiatry and the behavioral sciences," he stated.

For some, the words of William Faulkner would ring all too true: "The past is never dead, it is not even past."
