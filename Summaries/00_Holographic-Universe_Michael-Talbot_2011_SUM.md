## PART I

**Holographic Model of Reality and Memory Storage in the Brain**

**Penfield's Discoveries**:
- Belief that memories were localized in specific brain areas (engrams)
- Found that stimulating temporal lobes caused patients to reexperience vivid past memories
- Concluded everything experienced is recorded in the brain, including insignificant details

**Lashley's Research Challenges Penfield's Theory**:
- Failed to find evidence of engrams
- Rats retained memories despite removing various portions of their brains
- Suggested memories were distributed throughout the brain rather than localized in specific areas

**Pribram's Shift in Perspective**:
- Convinced by Lashley's findings that memories are not localized but distributed throughout the brain
- Seemed impossible to account for with existing mechanisms or processes
- Continued to ponder this idea, later proposing a holographic model of reality to explain it.

#### Pribram's Insight into Holographic Memory and Vision Processing:
- Pribram's puzzle: understanding how memories are distributed in the brain (1960s)
- Inspiration from holograms: interference pattern, three-dimensional images, ability to reconstruct whole image from small fragments

**Holography Basics:**
- Concept of holography: interference pattern created by two laser beams
- Laser light necessary for creating holograms: pure and coherent form of light
- Recording process: first beam bounces off object, second beam creates an interference pattern on film or plate
- Revealing the image: shinning another laser beam or bright light source through the film
- Three-dimensionality: convincing, but no physical object present

**Brain's Resistance to Surgical Excision:**
- Lashley's discovery: visual centers of brain surprisingly resilient to damage in rats and cats
- Complex visual tasks still performed despite significant losses
- Challenge to one-to-one correspondence theory between image seen and how it's represented in the brain

**Pribram's Experiments:**
- Inability to find one-to-one correspondence between electrical activity and images seen
- Hypothesis: vision might be holographic, as resistance to surgical excision suggested distributed processing
- Need for understanding wavelike phenomenon used in the brain for creating internal holograms.

### 2 The Cosmos as Hologram

**Bohm's Discovery of Holographic Universe**
- **Bohm's fascination with science**: as a young boy, he invented a dripless tea kettle and later became fascinated by quantum physics at Pennsylvania State College in the 1930s.
- **Quantum physics discoveries**: subatomic particles do not possess dimensions and can behave as both waves and particles. The only time quanta manifest as particles is when they are being observed.
- **Challenges to common sense**: quantum phenomena appear to defy common sense, suggesting a world ruled by sorcery rather than the natural world.
- **Interconnectedness of subatomic events**: Bohm found it perplexing that most physicists attached little importance to this phenomenon, which was later discovered to be hidden in one of quantum physics's basic assumptions.
- **Niels Bohr's assumption**: if particles only come into existence in the presence of an observer, it is meaningless to speak of a particle's properties and characteristics before observation.

#### Debate between Niels Bohr and Albert Einstein
**Background:**
- Science based on discovering properties of phenomena
- Bohr proposed that particle properties don't exist until observed (Copenhagen Interpretation)
- Implications for future of science unclear

**Einstein's Objections:**
- Famous 1935 paper "Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?"
- Two entangled particles can have identical properties, even at great distances (EPR Paradox)
- Special Theory of Relativity forbids faster-than-light communication between particles
- Rejected Bohr's interpretation as unreasonable and impossible

**Bohr's Response:**
- Subatomic particles don't exist until observed, not independent objects
- Particles are part of an indivisible system (interconnected)
- Most physicists sided with Bohr due to the success of quantum physics in predicting phenomena and lack of experimental evidence for EPR paradox.

**Bohm's Work:**
- Accepted Bohr's position but puzzled by lack of interest in interconnectedness
- Studied plasmas at University of California, Berkeley (1943)
  - Electrons behaved like part of a larger whole, producing organized effects
  - Called them "plasmons"
- Extended research to electrons in metals at Princeton University (1947)
  - Similar observations of collective behavior among vast numbers of particles.

**Bohm's Disillusionment:**
- Increasingly troubled by Bohr's interpretation of quantum theory
- Found interconnectedness crucial for understanding reality, but prevailing views ignored it.

#### Bohm's Search for Alternatives in Quantum Theory
- Decided to improve understanding of quantum physics by writing a textbook (1951)
- Felt uncomfortable with the theory, sought opinions from Bohr and Einstein
  - No response from Bohr
  - Einstein suggested they discuss the book
- Discussions with Einstein revealed shared dissatisfaction with the lack of deeper understanding in quantum theory
- Believed there was a deeper reality beyond the subatomic level, not yet discovered by science
- Proposed an alternative interpretation, focusing on a new type of field (quantum potential) on the subquantum level
  - Effects were subtle but powerful everywhere
  - Reception was negative, dismissed as heresy due to philosophical differences with Bohr's interpretation

**Bohm's Philosophical Arguments against Orthodox Quantum Theory**
- Challenge to completeness of any single theory in understanding an infinite universe
  - Nature may be infinite and cannot be fully explained by a single theory
- Expanded view on causality
  - An effect could have an infinite number of causes, not just one or several
  - Emphasized the interconnectedness of all events in the universe

**Bohm's New Approach: Wholeness and Nonlocality**
- Discovered that quantum potential emphasizes wholeness over parts
  - Behavior of parts organized by the whole system, not just interactions between them
  - Suggested that wholeness is more primary than classical view of a system as result of part interaction
- Implications for location: nonlocality at subquantum level
  - Contradiction to our everyday understanding of specific locations for objects.

#### Bohm's Nonlocality and Reality
- In the early 1950s, Bohm proposed a theory to complete quantum mechanics with unseen variables (impressed Bell)
- Nonlocality: particles interconnected despite being at different locations
  - No violation of special relativity
- Aharonov-Bohm effect (1952): electron feels magnetic field presence in forbidden region, challenging classical beliefs
  - Controversial discovery with limited acceptance

**Questions and Contributions:**
- Dissatisfaction with quantum theory
- Focus on order and degrees of disorder
  - Ordered vs. disordered objects
  - Degrees of order implied infinite hierarchies in the universe

**Inspiration from the Hologram:**
- Observation of a hologram on TV inspired Bohm to further develop his ideas on order and nonlocality.
- Glycerine experiment: drop of ink spread out but reconstituted, revealing hidden order within disordered state.
